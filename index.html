Meet your teacher

Hi, I’m Amy.

For more than 20 years I’ve been performing professionally and teaching private lessons, sectionals, and chamber ensembles. I’ve seen students of every age and level grow, and I’ve learned that the biggest breakthroughs rarely come from more hours in the practice room.

About 80% of learning music and performing is mindset.

That’s where my certification in life coaching comes in. Alongside technique and musicianship, I help you work through the things that really hold musicians back: performance nerves, perfectionism, self-doubt, and the stories we tell ourselves about what we “should” be able to do.

Swan Music Company is home to all of my music ventures: my teaching, my coaching, and Swantissimo, the horn and bassoon duo I share with my husband.

The approach

Music that fits your life

Every student is different. These are the ideas at the heart of how I teach.

𝄐

Practice smart, not hard

Long hours of mindless repetition aren’t the answer. You’ll learn focused, efficient practice strategies that get real results in the time you actually have.

✦

Mindset first

Confidence, focus, and a healthy relationship with mistakes. Life-coaching tools help you get out of your own way, in the practice room and on stage.

Play for joy

Joy looks different for everyone. It might be community band, chamber music with friends, an audition, or playing just for you. We’ll build toward what lights you up.

Coming back to music

It’s been a while? Welcome back.

Maybe you played all through school and then life got busy. Maybe your instrument has been sitting in a closet for ten, twenty, or thirty years. I specialize in helping people return to music gently, without judgment, and at a pace that feels good.

  • Rebuild technique and endurance without frustration
  • Set realistic, rewarding goals for your schedule
  • Quiet the inner critic that compares you to your younger self
  • Find your people: ensembles, community groups, and chamber partners

Good for you, too

The health benefits of making music

Playing music isn’t just fun. It’s one of the best things you can do for your mind and body, at any age.

Calmer mind

Focused playing can lower stress and gives your brain a welcome break from screens and to-do lists.

Sharper brain

Reading music, coordinating hands and breath, and listening all at once builds memory, attention, and mental agility.

Better breathing

Wind and brass playing strengthens breath support, posture, and core engagement in a way few other activities can.

Brighter mood

Making music releases feel-good chemistry and gives you a real sense of accomplishment, week after week.

Real connection

Ensembles, sectionals, and chamber groups bring people together and build friendships around something meaningful.

Lifelong growth

It’s never too late to learn. Music keeps you curious, challenged, and creative for life.
