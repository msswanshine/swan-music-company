Practice smart, not hard: 5 habits that actually work

By Amy · Swan Music Company

Most of us were taught that getting better means practicing longer. But hours alone don’t make great musicians. Focused, intentional practice does.

1. Start with a goal, not a timer

Before you play a note, decide what you want to be different by the end of the session. “Clean up the slur in measure 12” beats “practice for 45 minutes” every time.

2. Practice the hard part, not the whole piece

It’s tempting to start from the top and play until you stumble. Instead, find the trickiest few measures and give them your freshest energy. Then reconnect them to what comes before and after.

3. Slow down on purpose

Slow practice isn’t a punishment. It’s where your brain and body learn. Play it slowly and correctly a few times before nudging the tempo up.

4. Short and frequent beats long and rare

Twenty focused minutes most days will take you further than one marathon session on Sunday. Your brain does a lot of its learning between sessions.

5. Notice your self-talk

If the voice in your head is harsh, your playing will tighten up. Try speaking to yourself the way a supportive teacher would: curious, patient, and specific. Mindset is a skill you can practice too.

The goal isn’t to practice more. It’s to enjoy playing more and to get better along the way.
